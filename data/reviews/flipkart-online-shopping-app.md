## Summary

Flipkart's privacy policy reveals extensive data collection and broad sharing practices. The app gathers everything from browsing behavior and device fingerprints to government IDs and health prescriptions, then shares insights across a wide network of affiliates, advertisers, and lending partners.

Key concerns: broad data sharing with group companies and third-party advertisers, collection of PAN and KYC documents for credit profiling, behavioral tracking across websites, health data shared with teleconsultants, facial images stored for virtual try-on, and financial data used to qualify users for loans.

Key positives: users can opt out of personalized ads via device settings, a dedicated grievance officer is listed with contact details, and marketing emails can be unsubscribed.

## Summary Card

### Flipkart - Online Shopping App
- **Overall Risk:** Risky
- **Score:** 41/100 (Grade D)

**Red Flags:**
- **Collects government ID numbers.** Flipkart asks for your PAN card, Aadhaar, or other government-issued ID numbers. These are highly sensitive documents that can be used for identity theft if leaked.
- **Accesses your credit report.** To offer loans and PayLater services, Flipkart pulls your credit information report from credit agencies. This creates a financial profile about your borrowing habits.
- **Shares data across corporate group and partners.** Your shopping history, contact details, and financial data are shared with Flipkart's sister companies (Myntra, Cleartrip), lending partners, credit bureaus, advertisers, and even potential buyers if Flipkart is ever sold.
- **Collects health data for prescriptions.** When buying prescription medicines, Flipkart collects your medical information and may connect you with third-party doctors. Your health data could be shared with these teleconsultation providers.
- **Stores facial images for virtual try-on.** The "virtual try and buy" feature uploads photos of your face to Flipkart's servers. These images could be retained and used for purposes beyond just the try-on feature.
- **Tracks you across websites for ads.** Flipkart uses Google Analytics and third-party ad companies to track which websites you visit before and after Flipkart, building a detailed advertising profile about you.
- **Data sold in mergers or acquisitions.** If Flipkart merges with or is bought by another company, your personal data can be transferred to the new owner without requiring your fresh consent.

**Green Flags:**
- **Has a dedicated grievance officer.** Flipkart has appointed a Grievance Officer with a physical address and email (privacy.grievance@flipkart.com) for handling privacy complaints, which is a legal requirement in India.
- **Marketing emails can be opted out.** You can unsubscribe from promotional emails and SMS through the Notification Preference page in the app.
- **Personalised ad tracking can be disabled.** You can turn off personalised ad tracking through your phone's device settings using the "Opt out of Ads Personalization" feature.
- **Uses secure servers for transactions.** Flipkart claims to use secure servers and encryption when you access account information and make payments.

**Verdict:** Flipkart collects an unusually broad range of personal data for a shopping app, including government IDs, health information, and facial images, and shares it widely across its corporate family and with advertisers. The app is best avoided if you value keeping your shopping and identity data tightly controlled.
