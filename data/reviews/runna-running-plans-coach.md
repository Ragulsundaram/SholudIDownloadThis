## Summary

Runna is a well-structured running app owned by Strava with strong privacy rights and explicit health data protections. The app collects detailed workout metrics, GPS routes, and optional heart rate data. It shares information with Strava, advertising partners like Meta, and various service providers. On the positive side, Runna explicitly promises not to sell or use health data for advertising, provides prominent advance notice for material policy changes, and offers comprehensive user rights including data portability and permanent account deletion.

Key concerns: precise GPS is required for core features, data is shared with parent company Strava, targeted advertising through Meta, and activity data may be visible to other users.

Key positives: explicit promise not to use health data for ads, strong user rights with EU GDPR representative, no payment card storage, and opt-out for targeted advertising.

## Summary Card

### Runna: Running Plans & Coach
- **Overall Risk:** Caution
- **Score:** 79/100 (Grade B)

**Red Flags:**
- **Precise GPS required for core features.** To use the app for running, you must allow it to track your exact location using GPS. You can turn this off in your device settings, but then core tracking features will not work.
- **Data shared with parent company Strava.** Runna is owned by Strava. Your data is shared with Strava and its affiliates, who process it under their own privacy policies.
- **Targeted advertising via Meta.** Runna uses cookies and tracking tools to show you targeted ads through third-party networks like Meta. You can opt out, but some data sharing for advertising may still occur.
- **Activity data visible to other users.** Your runs, routes, photos, and comments may be visible to other Runna users depending on your privacy settings. Content shared publicly may also appear on other social media platforms.

**Green Flags:**
- **Health data never used for ads.** Runna promises not to sell or use health data such as heart rate for advertising, and will not share it with third parties without your explicit consent.
- **Prominent advance notice for policy changes.** If Runna makes important changes to its privacy policy, it will give you clear advance warning before those changes take effect.
- **Strong user rights and data portability.** You can access, correct, delete, restrict, and export your data. You can also withdraw consent at any time and delete your account permanently.
- **No payment card details stored.** Runna never collects or stores your credit card information. All payments are handled by secure third-party providers.
- **Opt-out for targeted advertising.** Runna provides a "Do Not Share My Personal Information" link and honors browser opt-out signals like Global Privacy Control.

**Verdict:** Runna is a well-structured running app owned by Strava with strong privacy rights and explicit health data protections. The app collects detailed workout metrics and GPS routes, and shares information with Strava and advertising partners. However, it explicitly promises not to use health data for ads, provides prominent advance notice for policy changes, and offers comprehensive user rights. Users who are comfortable with Strava's ecosystem and configure their sharing settings carefully will find this a reasonable privacy choice for a fitness app.
