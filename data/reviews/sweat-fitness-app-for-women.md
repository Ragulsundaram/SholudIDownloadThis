## Summary

Sweat is a popular women’s fitness app with strong user rights and transparent GDPR compliance, but its heavy reliance on ad tracking and broad third-party data sharing raises real privacy concerns. The app collects sensitive health data including height, weight, pregnancy status, and workout history. It may even infer medical conditions from user inputs. On the positive side, Sweat offers a full suite of privacy rights, does not store payment details, and provides an explicit opt-out for data "sales" under US state privacy laws.

Key concerns: extensive data sharing with Meta and other ad networks, targeted advertising using fitness data, sensitive health data collection, and public forum posts that remain indexed forever.

Key positives: strong user rights (access, deletion, export, consent withdrawal), GDPR compliance with EU representative, no payment data storage, and a dedicated "Do Not Sell My Information" opt-out.

## Summary Card

### Sweat: Fitness App For Women
- **Overall Risk:** Caution
- **Score:** 62/100 (Grade C)

**Red Flags:**
- **Shares data with Meta for ads.** Sweat and Meta are "joint controllers" of your data, meaning Meta receives your information to help deliver targeted ads to you across the internet.
- **Tracks you for targeted advertising.** Sweat uses cookies, pixels, and tracking tools from Meta, Google, TikTok, and Snap to follow your activity and show you personalized ads.
- **Collects sensitive health data.** The app collects your height, weight, pregnancy status, and workout history. It may even infer medical conditions or requirements from the information you provide.
- **Forum posts are public forever.** Anything you post in the Sweat forum becomes public information that can be viewed by anyone, even people outside the app. Search engines can index these posts, and they may remain visible even after you delete your account.
- **Shares data with authorities.** Sweat may share your personal data with government authorities, law enforcement bodies, and regulators when required by law or to enforce their legal rights.

**Green Flags:**
- **Offers "Do Not Sell" opt-out.** Sweat provides a dedicated webpage and cookie settings that let you opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of your personal data for advertising purposes.
- **Strong user rights and GDPR compliance.** You can request access to your data, correct it, delete it, export it, or restrict how it is used. European users benefit from GDPR protections and a local EU representative.
- **Never stores your payment details.** Sweat does not receive or store your credit card or payment information. All membership payments are handled entirely by third-party payment providers.
- **Can withdraw health data consent.** You can withdraw your consent for health data processing at any time within the app's Profile Menu. This won't stop you from using the app, though some features may not work properly.

**Verdict:** Sweat is a popular women’s fitness app with strong user rights and transparent GDPR compliance, but its heavy reliance on ad tracking and broad third-party data sharing raises real privacy concerns. The app collects sensitive health data including height, weight, pregnancy status, and workout history, and shares user data extensively with Meta, Google, TikTok, and Snap for targeted advertising. Consider using the opt-out tools and limiting health data sharing if you decide to install it.
