## Summary Card

**Overall Rating:** B (78/100) — **Caution**  
**Recommendation:** Recommended with caveats

**One-Liner:** Strong privacy commitments with local-first data storage, but AI features require sharing content with third-party providers.

### Red Flags
- **AI features process notebook content externally:** When you use Goodnotes AI features (like AI summaries, search, or meeting transcripts), your notebook content, conversation histories, and documents are processed by third-party AI providers like Amazon Web Services and Microsoft. This requires explicit opt-in consent, but means your private notes leave your device.
- **Many third-party service providers used:** Goodnotes shares your data with numerous third parties including Google Analytics, Mixpanel, Amplitude, AWS, Datadog, Mailchimp, Zendesk, and others for analytics, cloud storage, customer support, and marketing. While they claim not to sell data, the broad service provider network increases exposure.
- **Uses data for targeted advertising:** Goodnotes uses your data for marketing and targeted advertising, including "Customer Match" with Google to find similar audiences. While they claim to obtain consent for marketing cookies, they also rely on "legitimate interests" for personalization activities.
- **Cloud backup shares note content:** If you enable Goodnotes Cloud or iCloud sync, your notes and documents (including any personal data in them) are stored on third-party servers. While Goodnotes claims they don't access your notes, using cloud features means your content is no longer only on your device.

### Green Flags
- **Notes stay on your device by default:** Goodnotes stores your notes and documents locally on your device by default. They explicitly state they do not store your notes on their own servers and do not access or collect data from your notebooks without your permission.
- **Explicit no-sale data commitment:** The policy clearly states "We never sell or rent your data or notebook content to third parties." This is a strong privacy commitment that many apps don't make.
- **No biometric profiling from handwriting:** Goodnotes explicitly states they don't use your handwriting data to create individual digital templates or profiles for identifying you, so they don't consider this biometric data processing.
- **Strong security measures in place:** The policy describes technical safeguards including data encryption, secure servers, access controls, and breach notification procedures. They also limit access to personal data to those with genuine business need.
- **Comprehensive GDPR rights provided:** Goodnotes provides full GDPR rights including access, rectification, erasure, data portability, and objection rights. Users can delete data via the app and export their information.

### The Verdict
Goodnotes takes a privacy-conscious approach by keeping your notes on your device by default and explicitly stating they don't sell data. The app's local-first architecture means your private notes stay private unless you choose to enable cloud or AI features. However, enabling cloud sync, AI features, or certain integrations requires sharing data with multiple third-party service providers. The AI features in particular involve processing your notebook content through external AI models at Amazon Web Services and Microsoft. Users who want maximum privacy should avoid the AI features and keep their notes local-only without cloud sync.

## Category Breakdown

### Camera & Microphone — Caution
The app can access your microphone to record audio notes and meeting transcripts, but only when you actively choose to use these features. Audio recordings are stored locally unless you enable cloud sync. Microphone access is used for Audio Recordings and Meeting Notes features. Audio is transcribed using AI when enabled. Recordings sync to cloud only if iCloud or Goodnotes Cloud is enabled.

**Concerns:** AI transcription processes audio externally, transcripts may be stored on third-party servers

### Location & GPS — Caution
Goodnotes may collect your general location (like your time zone and country) from your device and IP address. This is used for service optimization and analytics, not for tracking your precise movements. Location data includes time zone settings and country derived from IP address and device settings.

**Concerns:** IP address may infer approximate location, location combined with other data for analytics

### Contacts & Phonebook — Safe
Goodnotes does not request or access your contacts or phonebook. Collaboration is done through shareable links, not by accessing your contacts list.

### Storage & File Access — Caution
Goodnotes needs file access so you can import PDFs, documents, and images into your notebooks. Your files are stored locally on your device by default, and the app can't access files you haven't explicitly imported. File access is core to the app's PDF annotation and document management features.

**Concerns:** Cloud sync exposes files to third-party infrastructure

### Data Sharing with 3rd Parties — Caution
Goodnotes shares data with many service providers (like AWS for cloud storage, Google Analytics for usage data, and Zendesk for support) but explicitly states they never sell your data or notebook content to third parties. Third parties include Google Analytics, Mailchimp, AWS, Datadog, Mixpanel, Amplitude, Paddle, HubSpot, Braze, Zendesk, and AI providers.

**Concerns:** Large number of service providers, AI features share content with external providers, Google Customer Match shares hashed customer lists

### Account & Identity Data — Caution
You need to create an account with your name and email to use Goodnotes. This is standard for most apps, and the information is used to manage your account and provide customer support.

**Concerns:** Email used for marketing if consent given, account data shared with analytics providers

### Behavioural & Ad Tracking — Caution
Goodnotes tracks how you use the app to improve their services and for marketing. They use tools like Google Analytics and Mixpanel, and run targeted ads with your consent. They also use "Customer Match" to find similar audiences on Google.

**Concerns:** Profiling for marketing purposes, Customer Match shares data with Google, legitimate interests used for some personalization

### Children's Data Handling — Safe
Goodnotes clearly states their app is not for children under 16. This is a responsible approach to protecting children's privacy.

### Network & Internet Activity — Caution
Goodnotes collects your IP address and network information when you use the app or website. This is used for security, service provision, and analytics.

**Concerns:** IP address can approximate location, activity logs linked to account

### Device Info & Fingerprinting — Caution
Goodnotes collects device information like your device type, unique identifiers, and storage usage to help the app work properly and for analytics.

**Concerns:** Unique device identifiers used for analytics, device info combined with other data for profiling

### Health & Biometric Data — Safe
Goodnotes does not collect health data or biometric information. They specifically state they don't use your handwriting to create biometric profiles for identifying you.

### Financial & Payment Data — Caution
If you make purchases, payment processing is handled by third-party processors (like Paddle). Goodnotes doesn't store your full credit card details, only limited information like your postal code and transaction history.

### Data Deletion & User Rights — Safe
Goodnotes provides strong data rights including the ability to access, delete, and export your data. You can delete your account and data through the app, and they comply with GDPR requirements.

### Policy Change Notification — Caution
Goodnotes may update their privacy policy and will notify you through the app, website, or by email. However, continued use of the app after changes counts as accepting the new policy.
