## Summary Card

**App:** Calm  
**Overall Risk:** Caution  
**Score:** 61 / 100 (Grade C)

### Red Flags
- **Sleep and mood data fuel ad targeting.** Calm asks about your sleep habits and moods during check-ins. While they say they don't diagnose you, this personal wellness data is collected alongside your browsing behavior and used to personalize ads you see on other apps and websites.
- **Email and phone converted to ad identifiers.** Calm's advertising partners can turn your email address or phone number into a unique tracking code. This lets them follow you across the internet and show you Calm ads based on your profile.
- **Employer told when you sign up.** If your Calm subscription comes from your employer or a family member's plan, Calm informs them that you signed up or redeemed the offer. They know you are using a meditation app.
- **Tracked across websites and apps for ads.** Third-party advertising companies use cookies, web beacons, and device identifiers to watch what you do on Calm and other websites. They use this to show you ads they think you will click.
- **Data stored in the United States.** Calm is a US company and processes your information there and in other countries. These places may not have the same privacy protections as your home country.

### Green Flags
- **Multiple ways to opt out of ad tracking.** You can opt out of targeted ads and data "sales" by visiting calm.com/optout, using the Cookie Preferences Manager, enabling a universal privacy signal on your browser, or changing your mobile device settings.
- **You can access, correct, or delete your data.** No matter where you live, you can ask Calm to show you what data they have, fix mistakes, export your data, or delete it. They also won't treat you differently for exercising these rights.
- **Claims not to infer health conditions.** Calm explicitly says it does not use your sleep data, mood check-ins, or health app information to guess or diagnose any medical or mental health conditions.
- **Data kept only as long as needed.** Calm says it keeps your personal data only for as long as necessary to run the service or meet legal requirements, and no longer.

### Verdict
Calm is one of the most popular meditation and sleep apps, but its privacy practices are a mixed bag. While it offers strong opt-out tools and transparent rights, it engages in extensive cross-platform ad tracking—converting your email and phone into ad identifiers, and using your wellness data to build a profile for targeted ads. For an app meant to help you relax and sleep, the level of behavioral advertising and employer data sharing is a meaningful downside.
