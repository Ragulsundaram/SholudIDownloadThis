## Summary Card

**Overall Risk:** Caution  
**Score:** 67 / 100 (Grade C)

### Red Flags
- **Identifiers used to track you across apps and websites:** The App Store label shows your device identifiers may be used to track you across apps and websites owned by other companies. The privacy policy explicitly mentions collecting your IDFA (advertising identifier).
- **Your activity is used to show you ads across the internet:** The app uses Google Ads, Facebook, and Twitter remarketing to show you ads on third-party websites after you visit the app, based on your past visits.
- **Detailed health and intimate data collected:** The app asks for health information including your weight, height, body temperature, menstrual cycle, symptoms, and even sexual activity details. This data is stored on their servers.
- **Data shared with multiple Google ad services:** The app shares data with Google Analytics, Google AdSense, DoubleClick, and AdMob. Google may use this data to personalise ads on its own advertising network.
- **Do Not Track signals are ignored:** The app explicitly states that its service does not respond to Do Not Track signals from your browser or device.

### Green Flags
- **Explicit no-sale commitment:** The app explicitly states it does not sell personal information. It also specifically says it never shares your tracked workout data with third-party advertisers.
- **Privacy policy changes notified in advance:** The app promises to notify you via email and/or a prominent notice on the service before any privacy policy change becomes effective.
- **GDPR and CCPA deletion rights available:** You can request access to, correction of, or deletion of your personal data. The app provides clear rights under both GDPR and CCPA.
- **Payment data handled by third-party processors:** The app does not store your payment card details directly. All payment processing is handled by PCI-DSS compliant third-party processors like Apple, Google, and PayPal.

### Verdict
Workout for Women collects extensive personal and health data including weight, height, body temperature, menstrual cycle, symptoms, and even sexual activity information. Your device identifiers are used to track you across other apps and websites for advertising. The app shares data with Google Analytics, Google AdSense, AdMob, and remarketing partners including Facebook and Twitter. On the positive side, the app explicitly states it does not sell personal data, does not share your tracked workout data with advertisers, provides GDPR and CCPA rights, and promises advance notice of privacy policy changes via email.
