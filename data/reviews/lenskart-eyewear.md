## Summary Card

### Lenskart: Eyewear
**Overall Risk:** Caution (Score: 59 / Grade: C)

**Red Flags:**
- **Facial scans stored for 5 years** — The app uses your phone's camera to scan your face for virtual try-ons, then keeps those scans and measurements on its servers for up to 5 years after you last use the service.
- **Tracks device and installed apps** — The app collects your device serial number, advertising ID, list of installed apps, and hardware model to track and identify your device.
- **Health data shared with third parties** — Your eye prescription and other health details are shared with third-party platforms and service providers to process and deliver your orders.
- **Behavioral tracking for ads** — The app uses advertising identifiers, cookies, and Google Analytics to monitor your behavior and send you promotional offers and advertising messages.
- **Data shared with affiliates and partners** — Your personal information is shared with company affiliates, delivery partners, and other third parties to fulfill orders and run promotions.

**Green Flags:**
- **No sale of personal data** — The company explicitly promises that it does not sell or rent your personal information to anyone.
- **Facial data not shared externally** — Photos and facial scans from the 3D try-on feature are not shared with any outside companies or advertisers.
- **User data rights available** — You can request to see, correct, or delete your personal data, and withdraw your consent, by contacting the company's data protection officer.
- **Encryption and security measures** — The company says it uses SSL encryption for payment information and restricts employee access to personal data on a need-to-know basis.

**Verdict:** Lenskart offers a convenient way to buy eyewear online with a popular virtual try-on feature, but its privacy practices come with notable trade-offs. The app collects sensitive facial and health data, tracks your device extensively for advertising, and shares your information with a broad network of affiliates and service providers. While the company claims not to sell data and provides some user rights, the long retention of biometric data and behavioral tracking mean privacy-conscious users should weigh the benefits carefully.

## Review Notes

- Re-scanned on 2026-05-18. Policy content unchanged from prior analysis.
- The financial data storage wording is ambiguous: the policy states card details "are stored on the Company's secure servers and/or in cookies on your computer." This may be a drafting error but warrants clarification.
- Location and contacts access are not mentioned in the policy, leaving them as Unknown and adding uncertainty to the score.
- The 5-year retention period for facial scans and health data is a significant concern given the sensitivity of biometric information.
- The collection of installed applications and advertising identifiers suggests active behavioral profiling, though there is no explicit mention of cross-app tracking.
- The NDNC section explicitly overrides do-not-call preferences for 365 days, which is aggressive but common in Indian e-commerce.
- User rights (access, correction, erasure, consent withdrawal) are well-documented and accessible via a dedicated Data Protection Officer email.
