# Canva: AI Video & Photo Editor — Privacy Review

**Analyzed:** 2026-05-18  
**Policy Version:** Last updated April 15, 2026  
**Policy URL:** https://www.canva.com/policies/privacy-policy/

---

## Summary Card

**Overall Risk:** Risky  
**Score:** 54/100  
**Grade:** D

### Red Flags

- **Heavy advertising tracking** — Canva tracks you across websites using cookies, device identifiers, and web beacons to show you personalized ads. They share your data with major ad networks like Facebook, Google, and LiveRamp to target you with ads on other sites.

- **Shares data with data brokers** — Canva buys information about you from third-party data providers (like your job title, company, and interests) and combines it with your Canva activity. They also share your email with ad partners to match you across different platforms.

- **Uses your content to train AI** — Your designs, uploads, and usage patterns may be used to train Canva's AI and machine learning models. While you can opt out, this is on by default and helps improve their AI features using your creative work.

- **Collects location for ads** — Canva collects your location from your IP address, GPS, and payment providers. They use this to personalize content and show you targeted advertisements based on where you are.

- **Persistent device tracking** — Canva collects device identifiers including your advertising ID that stay on your device even if you log out. These identifiers help track your usage patterns across sessions.

### Green Flags

- **Privacy controls available** — Canva provides settings to manage your privacy preferences, including options to opt out of third-party data collection, AI training, and personalized advertising.

- **Data deletion rights** — You can request access, correction, or deletion of your data by contacting Canva. They provide these rights for users in regions with privacy laws like GDPR and CCPA.

- **Student data protection** — Canva Education has special protections for students including no advertising, no AI training on student content, and COPPA/FERPA compliance.

### Verdict

Canva is a popular design tool with powerful features, but its privacy practices are concerning. The app collects extensive personal data and shares it widely with advertising partners and data brokers. While they provide privacy controls, the default settings expose users to significant tracking and profiling. Consider the privacy trade-offs carefully before using this app, especially for sensitive creative work.

## Category Breakdown

### Camera & Microphone — Caution

Canva accesses your camera and microphone only when you choose to take photos or record videos within the app. This is needed for the core photo editing features.

**Access:** On-demand, required for core features  
**Policy excerpt:** "We receive content that you create within Canva and media you upload, such as designs, images, documents, videos."

### Location & GPS — Risky

Canva collects your location from your IP address, when you enter it manually, and from payment providers. They use this to personalize content, target ads, and estimate taxes.

**Concerns:**
- IP-based location tracking is continuous
- Used for advertising purposes
- Shared with ad partners

**Policy excerpt:** "Canva may collect your precise or approximate location: from you, when you provide, correct or confirm your location; by inferring your location from your IP address; and from our partners or your payment provider."

### Data Sharing with 3rd Parties — Risky

Canva shares your information with many third parties including advertisers, data brokers, and service providers. They share your email with ad networks to match you across platforms and buy additional data about you from third parties.

**Concerns:**
- Email sharing for ad matching
- Data broker partnerships
- Extensive service provider access

**Policy excerpt:** "We partner with third party ad servers, ad networks and social media platforms to deliver personalized advertisements. Canva may share certain information with our third party advertising partners, such as your email address, location, cookie information."

### Account & Identity Data — Risky

Canva requires your name, email, and other personal details to create an account. They combine this with data from third parties to build a profile of you for advertising and link it to your activity across services.

**Concerns:**
- Profile enrichment from data brokers
- Linked to advertising identity

**Policy excerpt:** "We may combine information about you with information obtained from third-party sources to better understand your profile and interests so that we can deliver customized offers."

### Behavioural & Ad Tracking — Risky

Canva tracks everything you do in the app and on the web using cookies, device IDs, and web beacons. They use this to show you targeted ads and share your behavior with ad networks to target you on other websites.

**Concerns:**
- Cross-site tracking
- Device fingerprinting for ads
- Email matching with ad partners

**Policy excerpt:** "Cookies allow us or our business partners to track your usage of the Service over time. They also allow us to measure traffic and usage trends, deliver personalized advertisements, and measure their effectiveness."

### Device Info & Fingerprinting — Risky

Canva collects device identifiers including your advertising ID that persistently track your device. These IDs are used for advertising and may remain on your device even if you log out.

**Concerns:**
- Persistent advertising identifiers
- Device-level tracking

**Policy excerpt:** "Device identifiers are small data files that uniquely identify your mobile device. Device Identifiers used by Canva include the Android Advertising ID and iOS Advertising Identifier."

### Health & Biometric Data — Caution

Canva uses AI to analyze your photos for features like background removal and blemish correction. This involves detecting facial features and components in images you upload.

**Concerns:**
- Facial analysis using AI
- Part of ML training data

**Policy excerpt:** "Labeling and detecting components in images (e.g., background, eyes) in order to provide and enhance photo editing tools such as background removal, blemish and red eye correction."

### Data Deletion & User Rights — Caution

You can request deletion of your data, but Canva keeps some information for legal and backup purposes. Data shared with third parties cannot be deleted by Canva—you would need to contact those companies directly.

**Concerns:**
- Some data retained after deletion
- Third-party data not deletable by Canva

**Policy excerpt:** "Following termination or deactivation of your user account, Canva will retain your profile information and User Content for a commercially reasonable time. If you have shared your information with third parties, Canva will not be able to delete the information."
